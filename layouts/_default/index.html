I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science & Engineering (Robotics) at the University of Washington, where I study human-robot interaction with a core focus on accessibility. I am in the human-centered robotics lab advised by Maya Cakmak. My research is dedicated to building physically assistive systems that empower individuals. Currently, my work explores assisted play options for adults with cerebral palsy using mobile manipulator platforms.

I am currently an R&D intern at MIT Lincoln Laboratory in Group 103: Tactical Autonomy. Previously, I completed three software engineering internships at Apple (Watch Complications, Health Sensing Experience, Watch Frameworks), and gained research experience at NASA Ames Research Center working on situational awareness for off-nominal events.

Current Research & Projects

  • Human-Machine Teaming & Tactical Autonomy (MIT Lincoln Laboratory)
    Developing an open-world human-machine teaming testbed for pilots in training. This includes a replay visualizer for after-action explanation powered by LiteLLM. The chat assistant integrates context across radio, chat replay, and spatial view widgets, with scenarios generated by Synthex.
  • Humanoid Robots for In-Home Assistance
    Investigating privacy attitudes and user perceptions through Prolific user studies to understand requirements for deploying humanoid robots in home environments. (Work under review)
  • Assisted Play for Adults with Cerebral Palsy
    Investigating the Hello Robot Stretch RE3 mobile manipulator platform. Comparing functional versus physical tasks revealed higher error tolerance in play, shifting our focus toward activity-centered design and contextual inquiries. (Work under review)
