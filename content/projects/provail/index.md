## Overview
As part of my research in assistive robots for users with motor limitations, I collaborate with **PROVAIL** to conduct community deployments and exploratory user research. Our work investigates how mobile manipulator platforms, specifically the **Hello Robot Stretch RE3**, can be designed around real user agency, error tolerance, and recreational freedom.

---

## 1. Exploratory Studies & Formative Interaction Benchmarks
To understand how users interact with mobile manipulators in community day-center settings, we initially contrasted functional daily tasks against recreational play tasks using the Stretch RE3:
- **Functional Tasks**: Evaluated tasks such as opening and closing cabinet doors. 
- **Play Tasks**: Evaluated activities including a tabletop basketball hoop shot and ramp-assisted bowling.

- **Key Realization**: We discovered that **play was significantly preferred due to higher error tolerance**. In functional tasks, minor misalignments or execution errors can lead to frustration or safety hazards, whereas recreational contexts naturally embrace flexibility, competitive drive, and joyful engagement.

---

## 2. Situated In-Situ Play Deployments: Adapted Mini-Golf Course
Building directly on our exploratory findings regarding error tolerance and recreation, our study transitioned to a situated in-situ community deployment at PROVAIL featuring a custom-built, accessible mini-golf course.

- **Personalized Adaptation**: The mini-golf environment was integrated with participants' preferred accessibility modalities (such as touch input, switches, or eye-gaze dwell interfaces).
- **Community Deployments**: Conducted sessions where power wheelchair users played the adapted golf course using the mobile manipulator.
- **Key Findings**: As discussed in our HRI work, centering activities around inclusive play fostered deeper engagement, reduced anxiety around system errors, and provided rich qualitative insights into user-robot synergy, emphasizing material authenticity over screen-based alternatives.

## 3. Future Work: Adaptive Ability-Based Progressive Autonomy Interfaces
While our field deployments established robust insights into recreational interactions, the underlying interface work is still actively evolving. 
- **Current Direction**: I am currently developing adaptive, ability-based progressive autonomy interfaces for the Hello Robot Stretch RE3.
- **Goal**: Transition control granularity based on real-time user input preferences and functional ability profiles, so we can bridge the gap between high-level user intent and execution.
